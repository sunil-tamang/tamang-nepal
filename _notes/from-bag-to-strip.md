## Before the crochet

A material needs preparation before it can take on a different job. Discarded polythene bags are cleaned and cut into strips, ready for the slower work of crochet. Looking at what is available is part of deciding what comes next.

## One strip at a time

The strips are crocheted by hand, without heat. The colour already in the plastic becomes part of the new surface. Small differences in the starting material remain visible in the handwork.

## A construction, not one recipe

Crocheted plastic can be combined with fabric in the finished piece. The orange Chalk Bag, for example, has a fabric exterior with crocheted plastic within its structure. You cannot read every component from the outside.

## A separate beginning for cloth

Naturally dyed fabric follows another path. TAMANG buys fabric and works with a collaborator who dyes it with natural materials. That cloth is separate from the recovered plastic, even when the two appear in the same product.

## Ask about the piece

The useful questions are specific: which materials are used, how are they assembled, and what care do they need? A product’s construction tells a fuller story than the name of a single material.
